## Manuscript identity

The paper is best characterized as an interdisciplinary AI-governance / responsible-computing paper with a bounded controlled systems experiment. Its strongest contribution is not a new governance algorithm. It is a representation-and-evidence claim: upstream observation/representation choices and temporal state resolution can remain consequential through governed processing, replay, and reconstruction even while semantic interpretation remains unresolved.

## Candidate 1 — ACM FAccT 2027

**Fit:** Strong.

FAccT explicitly welcomes work on evaluations and evaluation practices, regulation and governance of computational systems, responsible/safe/ethical/trustworthy AI evaluation and governance, sociotechnical design, AI safety, transparency documentation, and related interdisciplinary work.

The current manuscript fits best if framed around:
- representation as a governance-relevant sociotechnical surface;
- independently preserved evidence chains;
- inspectable observation/interpretation/governance boundaries;
- cross-architecture evaluation without forced normalization;
- replay/reconstruction as accountability evidence.

**Current timing:** abstract deadline 2026-10-27; paper deadline 2026-11-03; conference 2027-06-21 through 2027-06-24.

**Submission implication:** a FAccT submission would require rapid completion of the literature/novelty matrix, anonymization, tighter engagement with responsible-computing literature, and a conference-style 12–14 page argument. The current paper is conceptually close but is not yet submission-ready.

## Candidate 2 — AI and Ethics

**Fit:** Strong journal fit.

The journal regularly publishes conceptual and applied work on AI ethics, governance instruments, socio-technical definitions, implementation practice, and human values. The manuscript's distinction between representation, interpretation, and governance fits that discourse, especially if the literature review makes clear which claims are established background and which empirical distinctions are contributed by the two experiments.

**Submission implication:** best suited if the authors want room for a fuller conceptual argument, expanded related work, methods, contribution statement, and limitations rather than compressing quickly to a conference deadline.

## Candidate 3 — Ethics and Information Technology

**Fit:** Moderate-to-strong, with a more philosophical framing.

The journal explicitly welcomes conceptual analysis of ethical ICT issues and dialogue between moral philosophy and information/communication technology. The manuscript could fit if revised to foreground epistemic/ethical consequences of transforming observation into machine-readable state and the accountability implications of hidden semantic authority.

**Submission implication:** would likely require deeper engagement with philosophy of information, epistemology, moral responsibility, and value-ladenness than the current systems-oriented draft contains.

## Candidate 4 — Journal of Responsible Technology

**Fit:** Moderate-to-strong applied fit.

The journal focuses on responsible development, deployment, and use of emerging technologies and is open to interdisciplinary responsible-technology research. The paper's controlled evidence chain and governance-oriented design implications fit an applied responsible-technology framing.

**Submission implication:** useful alternative if the authors want the experimental systems design and governance implications to stay more prominent than philosophical theory.
