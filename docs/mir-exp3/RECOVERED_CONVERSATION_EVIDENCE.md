MIR's future contribution could supply a third framework actor's participation history, testing whether commit-time standing relates to decision and residue, including a negative finding. Prefer completing the first experiment before the second.

**Richard, further refinement:** Reporting omission (something happened and nobody submitted it) differs from absolute observability limits (nothing any observer could have seen). MIR currently stores partner claims, not independent observations or physical-work measurements. Long participation histories may impose fabrication cost but cannot detect a compromised actor behaving consistently with its former pattern. In a manifold, preserve each observer's **unknowns** as first-class evidence, as well as disagreement; only a union of independently declared blind spots can describe what the participants disclose they cannot see, not unobservable domains beyond every participant.

**Richard, additional assessment:** Commitments show records unaltered but not completeness. A predeclared external coverage statement can make silence falsifiable; activity-side sampling (not just record-side sampling) can discover missing submissions. Separately attributable disagreement should survive rather than be flattened to apparent consensus.

**Richard, delivered PDF:** Corrected prior claim that three of four functions reduce to nothing: only Observe and Reconstruct; Retain and Demonstrate are substantive. MIR side is now written for independent checking, with explicit limitations alongside capabilities.
